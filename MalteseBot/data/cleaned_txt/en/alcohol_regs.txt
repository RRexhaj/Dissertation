CAP. 65 — TRAFFIC REGULATION ORDINANCE: DRIVING UNDER THE INFLUENCE OF DRINK OR DRUGS (ARTS 15–15G)
Laws of Malta, Chapter 65 (Traffic Regulation Ordinance)

15. PRESCRIBED ALCOHOL LIMITS
15(1) It is an offence to drive, attempt to drive, or be in charge of a motor vehicle on a road or other public place after consuming alcohol such that the proportion of alcohol in the breath, blood or urine exceeds the prescribed limit.
15(2) The prescribed limits are:
  (a) General drivers (all vehicles except those in (b), (c) and (d)): 22 microgrammes of alcohol per 100 millilitres of breath, OR 50 milligrammes per 100 millilitres of blood (0.05 g/100ml), OR 67 milligrammes per 100 millilitres of urine;
  (b) Drivers of commercial vehicles: 9 microgrammes per 100 millilitres of breath, OR 20 milligrammes per 100 millilitres of blood (0.02 g/100ml), OR 27 milligrammes per 100 millilitres of urine;
  (c) Drivers of buses, coaches, vehicles on priority duty and other vehicles carrying passengers for a fee: zero — 0 microgrammes of breath alcohol and 0 milligrammes in blood or urine;
  (d) Holders of a probationary driving licence (new drivers): 9 microgrammes per 100 millilitres of breath, OR 20 milligrammes per 100 millilitres of blood (0.02 g/100ml), OR 27 milligrammes per 100 millilitres of urine.
15(3) The Minister responsible for the police may, by regulations, change the prescribed limits.

15A. BREATH TEST POWERS
15A(1) A police officer or community officer may require a person who is driving, attempting to drive, or in charge of a motor vehicle on a road or other public place to provide a specimen of breath for a breath test.
15A(2) The officer may require a breath test where they reasonably suspect that the person has alcohol in their body, has committed a traffic offence while the vehicle was in motion, or has been involved in an accident.

15B. EVIDENTIAL SPECIMENS
15B(1) Where a preliminary breath test is positive, an evidential specimen of breath may be required using an approved device, or alternatively a specimen of blood or urine for laboratory analysis.
15B(2) A specimen of blood may only be taken by a medical practitioner, and the person is entitled to a portion of any blood or urine specimen for independent analysis.

15G. FAILURE OR REFUSAL TO PROVIDE A SPECIMEN
15G(1) A person who, without reasonable excuse, fails or refuses to provide a specimen of breath, blood or urine when lawfully required commits an offence.
15G(2) Such refusal is treated as equivalent to exceeding the prescribed alcohol limit and is punished accordingly.

OFFENCES, PENALTIES AND DRUGS
- A person who drives or is in charge of a motor vehicle while exceeding the prescribed limit, or while under the influence of a drug, commits a criminal offence punishable by a fine, penalty points, licence disqualification and, in serious cases, imprisonment.
- For tetrahydrocannabinol (cannabis) the prescribed limit is 2 microgrammes per litre of blood.
- A second or subsequent conviction within a short period carries higher penalties and longer disqualification.
- Drink- or drug-driving that causes injury or death is prosecuted as a serious criminal offence.
